Content creators (in the widest sense) hold these truths to be self-evident, but you *may-not* (yet!) 😃.

When you commission content (in the widest sense), you *MUST* understand that content creators don't do magic, that technical documentation is a co=creative process and that content creators need guidance/direction from you to effectively deliver that content.

**VISION**  
You *MUST* have the vision to 'elevator pitch' the scope of the content - for example - "*to document all the knowledge and how-to steps that Field Engineers will need to install our products in customer sites*" and follow up with a detailed list of what that *knowledge* is and what those *how-to steps* are.

You *MUST* also understand that the better you understand what your content requirements are, the better the content creator can deliver against those requirements.

**SCOPING**  
The content creator needs to know (from you) what to document, and what not to document.

**PRIORITISATION**  
The content creator needs to know (from you) what the most-important documentation tasks are, and what the least-important documentation tasks are.

**GUIDANCE**  
The content creator needs guidance from someone with specific knowledge about each specific deliverable.

**PERSPECTIVE**  
The content creator is helped by knowing more about what is going on around them (than you might imagine they need to know. Keep them in the loop.

**ADDING VALUE**  
The content creator can add value without **Direction**, **Guidance**, or **Perspective**, but the less-support the content creator gets, the less-efficient their writing process will be.

**TABLE OF CONTENTS**  
In the past, I have suggested that **Clients** create a table of contents (like) outline for them to 'work through' what they want to be documented. 
This can be a 'working document', starting with the highest-level headings, but it must eventually get to the detail of:
- *"What tools you will need to install a [component] to a [unit]"*
- *"How to install a [component] to a [unit]"*
